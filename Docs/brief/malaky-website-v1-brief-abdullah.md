**MALAKY**

**Website V1 — Required Changes & Creative Direction**

*Implementation brief for design & development team*

# Executive Direction

Do not rebuild the current website. Keep the premium visual language, warm neutral palette, generous whitespace, rounded surfaces, and the existing scroll-linked 3D movement. The primary change is the visual storytelling.

**Core decision: **replace the admin-dashboard-centric 3D visual with floating marketing outputs. The product interface supports the story; the marketing Malaky creates is the hero.

# 1. Navigation

Keep: How Malaky works · Pricing · FAQ · Sign in · Start free

Changes:

Increase the Arabic ملاكي logo approximately 20–25%.

Do not add the English word “Malaky” beside the Arabic mark.

Keep the navigation warm ivory with charcoal text and a charcoal primary CTA.

Do not add gradients, a gold navigation bar, or additional menu items.

# 2. Hero — Replace the Positioning

**REMOVE:**

*Your marketing team's AI co-pilot*

**REPLACE WITH:**

**Your marketing, already done.**

Malaky learns your business and prepares your marketing before you ask. You review. You approve. Malaky handles the rest.

Primary CTA: Start free
Secondary CTA: See how it works →

Remove the large duplicate Arabic logo from inside the hero panel. The navigation already carries the brand mark.

# 3. Hero Visual — Highest Priority Change

Keep the current floating 3D concept and scroll movement, but stop using the admin dashboard as the main object.

Replace it with a floating 3D stack of 5–7 realistic marketing assets:

Instagram — visual campaign + caption preview

LinkedIn Company — company announcement + branded graphic

LinkedIn Executive — CEO/personal-brand post

Arabic Social — native RTL campaign with real artwork

Newsletter — branded email layout with headline and CTA

Optional: X / Threads short-form post

Malaky's environment should remain neutral (ivory, charcoal, stone, restrained gold). Customer marketing should use different customer brand colors. This demonstrates that Malaky learns each customer's identity.

Headline: “Built overnight.” Supporting copy: “Every morning, your marketing is already waiting.” Show Instagram, LinkedIn, newsletter, Arabic and CEO content clearly.

The approved cards move toward their relevant channels. The visual should communicate that Malaky handles the next step after approval.

# 6. How Malaky Works

**01 — Introduce your business** — Tell Malaky about your brand, products, audience and goals. Once.

**02 — Malaky learns** — Your brand voice, preferences, calendar and approvals become part of its memory.

**03 — Malaky prepares** — Content and campaigns are created proactively around your business and what's coming next.

**04 — You review** — Everything that needs your attention arrives in one workspace.

**05 — You approve** — Edit, approve or decline. Nothing publishes without you.

**06 — Malaky keeps working** — Tomorrow, it happens again.

# 8. Middle East Positioning

**Headline: Arabic, natively.**

Not translated. Written for how people and businesses actually communicate across the region.

**Secondary message: Built around your calendar.**

Show regional calendar awareness through product examples: Ramadan, Eid al-Fitr, Eid al-Adha, Saudi National Day, UAE National Day, Jordan Independence Day, etc.

Avoid cliché regional decoration. Do not rely on mosques, lanterns, dunes or generic Arabic patterns to signal the Middle East.

# 9. Trust & Factual Accuracy

**Headline: AI shouldn't invent your business.**

Malaky works from your approved business information and trusted sources. Factual claims remain traceable, and nothing publishes without your approval.

# 13. Motion Standard

Keep motion slow, premium and scroll-led. Cards may float slightly, separate, change depth, rotate subtly, come forward when relevant and reorganize as the story progresses.

Avoid bouncing, fast spinning, particles, exploding cards and excessive parallax.

# FULL-SITE REVIEW ADDENDUM — FINAL V1 DIRECTION

**Objective: **Turn the current site from a polished AI SaaS landing page into a distinctive Malaky product story: proactive, brand-aware, approval-controlled, Arabic-native, and visibly focused on finished marketing output.

# 19. What the Full-Site Review Changes

The current visual foundation is strong. Do not redesign from zero.

The lower half becomes too text-heavy and static after the 3D hero. Introduce product proof, content examples, motion, and stronger visual hierarchy.

The product must be sold around outcomes and workflow, not AI models, credits, or internal implementation terminology.

The site should repeatedly show the output Malaky creates: social posts, executive LinkedIn content, Arabic campaigns, newsletters, calendars, approvals and learning.

Every section must answer one of five questions: What does Malaky make? Why is it different? Why can I trust it? Why is it built for me? What do I do next?

# 20. Replace the Current 'How Malaky Works' Section

The current three text rows are too plain and undersell the product. Replace them with a visual 5-step workflow.

**1. Introduce your business once**
Logo, brand colors, products/services, audience, goals, tone, approved sources and key dates.

**2. Malaky learns your brand**
It builds persistent brand memory from your information, preferences and approvals.

**3. Your marketing is prepared**
Posts, campaigns and executive content appear proactively around your schedule and upcoming opportunities.

**4. You review and approve**
Edit, approve or decline. Nothing goes out without your sign-off.

**5. Malaky publishes and learns**
Approved content is scheduled/published to supported channels and future drafts improve from your decisions.

Design: horizontal/scrolling sequence on desktop; stacked cards on mobile. Each step should have a small live product visual rather than a generic icon.

# 21. Memory — Make This a Signature Product Moment

Keep the concept, but the current example looks like a writing-rule editor. It should communicate persistent business intelligence.

Headline: Malaky remembers your business.

Copy: Teach Malaky once. Your brand voice, products, audience, offers, approved facts, preferences and past decisions become the starting point for every future draft.

Brand voice — learned

Products & services — remembered

Audience — remembered

Approved facts & sources — remembered

Content preferences — learned

Past approvals — learned

Upcoming campaigns & dates — tracked

Add a small 'Memory updated from your approval' animation after an approval interaction. This makes the learning loop tangible.

# 22. One Brand, Every Channel — Replace Generic Integration Cards

The current Facebook / Instagram / LinkedIn / X boxes look like an integrations page. Turn this into a content transformation demonstration.

Headline: One idea. Made right for every channel.

Copy: Malaky keeps the same brand and message, then adapts the format, length and creative for each channel.

Instagram: visual-first branded campaign card

LinkedIn Company: business update with branded graphic

LinkedIn Executive: personal thought-leadership post

Facebook: localized customer-facing post

Arabic social: native RTL version, not a translated English layout

Newsletter/email: longer-form campaign or update

Show actual platform names/logos subtly on each output card. Do not imply unsupported publishing integrations. Label roadmap channels clearly.

# 23. Human Approval — Convert Text Into Product Proof

Current section has too much empty space and no proof. Replace with a large approval interaction.

Headline: Nothing goes out without you.

Copy: Malaky does the work. You keep the final say.

Show one realistic post with: Approve · Edit · Decline · Source / Why this was suggested.

After approval, animate the card into a 'Scheduled' state. This visually closes the control loop.

# 24. Built for the Middle East — Strengthen the Moat

The current section is directionally right but too generic. The Arabic logo alone is not enough.

Headline: Built here. Written for here.

Primary copy: Malaky understands the language, business rhythm and calendar of the region — from native Arabic and RTL creative to the occasions that shape what your customers care about.

Use three visual proof blocks:

Arabic, natively — Arabic copy and layouts created as Arabic, not translated after the fact.

Your country's calendar — Ramadan, Eid, national days, launches, seasons and business-specific dates.

Local timing & context — content prepared around the market, timezone and audience.

Use an elegant split-screen English/Arabic campaign example. Avoid decorative Middle East clichés.

# 25. Add Personal & Executive LinkedIn

This is a meaningful differentiator and is missing from the current lower site.

Headline: Your company has a voice. So do the people behind it.

Copy: Malaky can prepare company content and personal LinkedIn content for founders, executives and sales leaders — each with its own voice and goals.

Visual: company post on one side; CEO/founder post on the other, both derived from the same business event but written differently.

# 26. Add the Proactive Calendar / Opportunity Engine

This is one of the strongest reasons Malaky is not simply another AI writing interface. Give it its own section.

Headline: Malaky knows what's coming.

Copy: Important dates should not depend on someone remembering to prompt an AI tool. Malaky watches your calendar and prepares relevant campaigns before the opportunity arrives.

Visual: elegant calendar/timeline with an upcoming event and a draft already marked 'Prepared'.

Do not claim autonomous knowledge of an event unless the product actually has the calendar/source data required to support it.

# 27. Trust & Sources

Keep the source-traceability concept but make it concrete.

Headline: Marketing without made-up facts.

Copy: Malaky creates from the business information and sources you trust. When a factual claim matters, you can see where it came from before you approve it.

Visual: post preview + small source drawer. Avoid broad claims such as 'every fact is sourced' unless the implementation guarantees that behavior.

# 28. Pricing — Major Change Required

The current pricing is the weakest strategic section on the site.

Remove customer-facing language about 'Balanced drafting model', 'Creative + Precise drafting models', 'all models' and similar model-selection terminology.

Do not make AI model access a core plan differentiator. Customers are buying a marketing outcome.

Reconsider exposing 'credits' as the headline usage mechanism. Credits create cognitive load and make Malaky feel like a generic AI generator.

If credits are technically necessary, keep them in account usage/billing rather than making them central to the marketing page.

Do not promise 'All channels' if some channels are still roadmap.

If the product is not truly ready for self-service, do not offer a permanent $0 plan simply to match SaaS convention.

Recommended packaging direction for launch:

| Plan | Best for | Outcome-led packaging | CTA |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Starter | Solo / small business | Core brand memory, planned monthly content, connected channels available at launch, approval workflow | Start free / Start trial |
| Growth | Growing business | More brands/channels/content volume, advanced calendar, executive LinkedIn, analytics | Start trial |
| Business | Teams / multi-brand | Multiple users/brands, approvals, higher volume, priority support, advanced controls | Talk to us |

Final price points should be based on actual generation cost, support cost, target customer willingness-to-pay and launch economics. Do not lock the public page to $29/$79 simply because they look familiar.

# 29. FAQ — Expand From Utility to Objection Handling

Rename 'Questions people ask' to 'Frequently asked questions' or simply 'FAQ'. Add questions that close real buying objections:

Does Malaky publish without my approval?

Will Malaky remember my brand and business information?

Can it write Arabic natively?

Can it create content for my personal LinkedIn as well as my company?

Which channels can Malaky publish to today?

What happens when a channel is not connected?

Where does Malaky get factual information about my business?

Can my team review and approve content?

Can I edit a draft before publishing?

How is my company data protected?

Can I cancel or change plans?

# 30. Final CTA — Keep the Dark Moment, Upgrade the Message

The dark final CTA is a strong visual break. Keep it.

Replace headline with: Tomorrow's marketing is already waiting.

Supporting copy: Set up your brand once. Malaky starts preparing what comes next.

Primary CTA must match the rest of the site: Start free OR Request early access — never alternate between launch models.

Remove the repeated admin-dashboard image behind this CTA. Use the floating content-output system or a quiet abstract Malaky surface instead.

# 31. Sitewide UX / Visual Polish

Use the Arabic ملاكي logo consistently and give it more breathing room. Keep the exact calligraphic details intact.

Introduce the champagne-gold accent selectively: active states, tiny dividers, status details, CTA moments and the final CTA. Do not turn the UI gold.

After the hero, alternate between product visuals and concise copy. Avoid consecutive text-only sections.

Use real-looking sample businesses and outputs consistently across the site rather than unrelated placeholder examples.

Create 3–4 persistent demo brands (for example logistics, hospitality/restaurant, retail, professional services) and reuse them throughout the story. This makes the site feel like one product demo rather than a collage.

Every mock post must look publication-ready. No blank colored rectangles.

Ensure Arabic samples use correct RTL hierarchy, punctuation and alignment.

Use micro-motion to show status transitions: Prepared → Needs review → Approved → Scheduled → Published.

Mobile must preserve the story. 3D cards should simplify into a swipe/stack experience rather than disappearing.

Add reduced-motion support for accessibility and performance.

Optimize the 3D/scroll experience aggressively for Core Web Vitals; visual ambition cannot make the site slow.

# 32. Product Experience Standards Beyond the Landing Page

To make the app itself competitive, the website promise and product must converge on these V1 product pillars:

**Brand Brain** — Persistent memory for brand voice, products, offers, audiences, approved sources, forbidden claims, visual identity and past decisions.

**Morning Workspace** — A single prioritized queue showing what Malaky prepared, what needs a decision and what is already scheduled.

**Proactive Planner** — Calendar and opportunity engine that surfaces upcoming occasions and prepares content before deadlines.

**Multi-Voice Content** — Separate company, founder/executive and campaign voices under the same workspace.

**Native Arabic System** — Arabic-first generation and RTL creative templates, not translation as an afterthought.

**Approval Guardrail** — No publishing without configured approval; clear edit/decline/reason workflows.

**Source Confidence** — Trace important factual claims to approved business data/sources and expose uncertainty instead of inventing.

**Channel Adaptation** — Transform one marketing idea appropriately for each supported channel instead of copy-pasting identical text.

**Learning Loop** — Use approvals, edits and declines as preference signals so future work becomes more aligned.

**Performance Feedback** — Bring post results back into Malaky so future recommendations can learn from what actually worked.

**Malaky should not look or sound like an AI content generator.**

It should feel like a proactive marketing operating system for businesses in the Middle East: it remembers the business, anticipates the calendar, creates finished channel-ready marketing, brings decisions to the user, keeps humans in control, and improves from what they approve.

**The product promise in one line: **Introduce your business once. Wake up to marketing that's already done.
